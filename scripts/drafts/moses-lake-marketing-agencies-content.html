Why Moses Lake's Marketing Agency Market Stays Small

Moses Lake has grown fast by small-town standards — the city's population has climbed to roughly 26,969 as of a 2024 U.S. Census Bureau estimate, up from around 10,000 in the mid-1980s, while Grant County as a whole has grown from about 50,000 residents in 1985 to more than 105,000 in 2025. That's real, sustained growth. What it hasn't produced is a deep bench of full-service advertising agencies, and the reason comes down to what the local economy actually looks like.

Agriculture, forestry, and fishing is Grant County's single largest employment sector, accounting for roughly 9,326 of the county's 43,169 average annual jobs in 2024. Per the USDA Census of Agriculture, Grant County is Washington's top producer of both vegetables and cattle and calves, and its second-highest producer of fruit. That's a heavily B2B, heavily industrial employer base — packing houses, feedlots, equipment dealers — not the dense cluster of consumer-facing retail and hospitality brands that tends to support a deep bench of advertising agencies in a market this size.

There's a genuinely unusual second layer to the local economy, too. Grant County International Airport sits on the site of the former Larson Air Force Base, active from 1948 and originally built in 1942, and its 13,500-foot runway is the 17th-longest in the United States. Today it functions as a major flight-test site: Boeing uses the airfield, through Million Air Moses Lake, for testing on the 777X, 737 MAX 10, 787 Dreamliner, and 747-8 programs. That gives a town this size an aerospace-testing dimension most comparable markets simply don't have, alongside notable local employers like REC Silicon, Genie Industries, Basic American Foods, International Paper, J.R. Simplot Co., and National Frozen Foods, with Big Bend Community College serving as the area's main workforce-training institution.

Put those two things together — a small population base and an employer mix that's agricultural and industrial rather than consumer-brand-heavy — and the honest result is exactly what this research found: demand skews toward digital-marketing generalists, web designers, and print/graphic-design shops, not a deep roster of full-service advertising agencies. That's not a knock on the town; it's just what the market actually supports, which is why the honest count here is two, with several genuinely real, long-running local businesses falling just short of "full-service" scope.

What "Full-Service" Actually Means Here

Part of why this list is so short is that "full-service marketing agency" gets used loosely across directories, self-descriptions, and even some agencies' own homepages. For this research, full-service meant genuine evidence of work across more than one discipline — not just a services page listing SEO, social, and branding as bullet points with no proof any of it happens beyond the agency's own website. MOSEY Marketing's scope, including PR and crisis management alongside the more common digital disciplines, is the broadest found anywhere in Moses Lake. GridPoint Marketing's scope is real but self-reported as "full service" while every verifiable signal points to a digital-only practice — which is exactly the kind of gap between branding and substance this whole exercise was built to catch, in both directions.

What to Check Before Hiring Either Agency

  • Ask directly what "full service" includes — get a specific list of disciplines the team has actually delivered, not just a services page
  • Confirm the current address and phone number yourself before signing anything, especially given how often mail-drop suites and out-of-town area codes turned up in this research
  • Ask who specifically will work your account, and whether that person has done the type of work you actually need
  • Get a case study or reference at your budget level — neither company publishes a rate card, so there's no way to compare pricing without asking directly
  • Clarify any leadership or founding-date discrepancy directly — if a detail is inconsistent across public sources, a legitimate business should be able to explain it plainly
  • Request contract length and exit terms in writing before any retainer begins
  • Verify Washington business registration yourself through the Washington Secretary of State's business search if the engagement size warrants it
