CityScope Net — The One Verified Web Design Company in Pasadena, TX

CityScope Net is located at 3713 Edgefield Drive, Pasadena, TX 77503, an address independently confirmed across five unrelated sources: the company's own site, ThreeBestRated, Expertise.com, TrustAnalytica, and its LinkedIn profile — all in agreement, well past the two-source bar this series applies. The company is owned by Ingrid Kast Fuller, making it a woman-owned business, and its roots go back further than almost any other name that turned up in this search.

CityScope Net's history is genuinely unusual for a business this size. It began in 1983 as a bulletin board system (BBS) Fuller ran out of her garage, formally launched as "CityScope Computer Services" on December 31, 1984, and was renamed "CityScope Net" on June 30, 1995 when it became an internet service provider. That makes it one of the longest continuously operating local tech businesses in Pasadena, predating the commercial internet itself by more than a decade.

An address history worth untangling. One older directory, iBegin, cites a "4808 Fairmont Pkwy #283" address for the company — a listing that's stale rather than wrong outright. CityScope Net's own history page confirms three sequential Pasadena locations before it settled where it is now: Southmore Ave. from 1992 to 1995, an expanded Southmore location from 1995 to 1999, and the Fairmont Pkwy address from 1999 to 2001, when the retail storefront closed as the business consolidated to remote and office-based operations. 3713 Edgefield Drive is the correct, current address, and it's the one confirmed across the five sources named above — the Fairmont Pkwy listing simply never got updated after the company moved on.

Why Pasadena's Web Design Market Comes Down to One Company

Pasadena, Texas isn't a small town wanting for local businesses. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, its population was estimated at 149,615 in 2024, and its 2024 Business Census shows Retail Trade (9,208 employees), Construction (9,006), and Manufacturing (7,239) as its three largest employment sectors. Median household income sat at $64,270 as of 2023. That's a real, working local economy — just not the kind that historically sustains a bench of boutique web design studios.

The city sits directly on the Houston Ship Channel, home to the Chevron Pasadena Refinery and the Chevron Phillips Chemical Pasadena Plastics Complex. That petrochemical and industrial base shapes the kind of small businesses Pasadena actually has in volume: contractors, industrial suppliers, trade shops, and service companies, rather than a dense cluster of consumer-facing retail or professional-services firms that tend to generate steady local demand for custom web design work. It also explains why so much of this search resolved to out-of-state or Houston-metro agencies running a Pasadena-targeted page rather than a real local bench — roughly half of all "web design Pasadena TX" results traced back to agencies with zero physical presence in the city at all.

CityScope Net's own client list is a genuine, almost checklist-perfect match for that industrial and trade-heavy economy: temporary staffing, pest control, industrial valves and instruments, transmission repair, glass and mirror work, and pool construction. That's not a coincidence so much as a real fit — a long-tenured local generalist that grew up serving exactly the kind of blue-collar and industrial small-business economy Pasadena actually has, rather than a boutique studio built around a client type the city doesn't have much of. Pasadena's civic life outside that industrial base includes long-running events like the Pasadena Strawberry Festival and the Pasadena Little Theatre, both signs of a genuine, functioning small-city community — just one whose commercial center of gravity sits with heavy industry, not with the kind of retail and services density that tends to produce a deep web design agency bench.

What to Check Before Hiring a Web Designer in Pasadena, TX

  • Confirm the address independently — search the company name plus its exact street address across at least two unrelated sources (Google Business Profile, a review platform, LinkedIn, a state registry) rather than trusting a single "About" page
  • Check whether web design is a real, dedicated service or a single line item — a company with a full page describing its build process, technology, and features is a different kind of partner than one that mentions "websites" once in a long services list
  • Ask directly about a Better Business Bureau profile if you don't find one — a missing BBB listing isn't automatically disqualifying for a long-running local business, but it's worth a direct conversation
  • Treat third-party pricing data as a starting point, not a quote — get an itemized, written scope and price directly from the company before signing anything
  • Ask who owns the finished site, the domain, and the hosting account — all three should transfer to you, not stay locked inside the designer's own accounts
  • Make sure your Google Business Profile stays under your own login, never created or held inside the agency's account
  • Widen the search radius if you need deep specialization — a thin local market is a legitimate reason to consider a well-vetted Houston-metro provider, as long as you apply the same address- and service-verification standard used throughout this post
