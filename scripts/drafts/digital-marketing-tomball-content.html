What to Check Before Hiring a Digital Marketing Agency

  • Confirm the specific digital channel you need is a real, dedicated service — ask to see actual ad-account or social-account examples, not just a service list on a website
  • Verify the address independently — check the agency's stated address against at least one Chamber of Commerce listing or third-party directory before assuming a website's own claims are enough
  • Ask who actually manages your account day-to-day — a small, full-service shop may have one or two people covering ads, social, and design simultaneously, which isn't disqualifying but is worth knowing upfront
  • Press on self-reported statistics — client counts, retention percentages, and review averages quoted on an agency's own site should be treated as claims to verify, not settled facts
  • Check for a BBB profile or rating — none of the three companies profiled here carries a discoverable BBB rating, which isn't automatically disqualifying, but it means you're relying more heavily on direct references and independent reviews
  • Get ad-spend and management-fee terms in writing separately — a management fee on top of ad spend is standard, but the split should be explicit before you sign
  • Clarify who owns the ad accounts and page assets — your Google Ads account, Meta Business Manager, and social pages should be owned by you, not locked inside the agency's own management account
