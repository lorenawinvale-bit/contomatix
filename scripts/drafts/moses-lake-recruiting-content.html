Why Moses Lake's Recruiting Market Looks the Way It Does

Moses Lake has grown fast. The city sits at roughly 26,969 residents as of 2024, up from around 10,000 in the mid-1980s — nearly tripling in four decades — and it anchors a Grant County population that's now estimated at 105,727 as of 2025. That growth alone would make Moses Lake a more competitive hiring market than its small-town reputation suggests, but the real story is what the local economy is actually built on, because it's really two economies running side by side.

The first is agriculture, and it's not a minor sideline. Grant County has 1,858 farms producing crops and livestock valued at $1.19 billion annually, and food processing built around that agricultural base employs more than 2,100 people in a $364 million industry. Lamb Weston BSW, J.R. Simplot Co., and National Frozen Foods all run major processing operations in Moses Lake itself, with ConAgra Foods and Quincy Foods nearby adding to the region's processing capacity. That's a labor market with real seasonal swings and a constant need for farm-labor contracting, food-processing staff, and the clerical and logistics work that supports both.

The second economy runs through Grant County International Airport, the former Larson Air Force Base, whose runways — some over 13,000 feet — make it one of the country's premier flight-testing sites; AeroTEC operates major flight-test facilities there. Big Bend Community College feeds that pipeline directly, training commercial pilots and aviation maintenance technicians who move straight into local aerospace and aviation employers. That's an entirely separate, non-agricultural labor-demand driver sitting right next to the farm economy.

This dual economy is exactly why the four agencies that cleared verification below aren't four interchangeable generalists: one focuses on office and light-industrial work, one is a general HR and staffing generalist serving Columbia Basin employers broadly, one is a dedicated healthcare-staffing specialist, and one is built specifically around farm and agricultural labor. That's genuine category diversity driven by genuine local economic diversity, not four names padded out to hit a round number.

How This List Was Verified

Every firm on this list had to clear the same bar: a real, staffed office physically inside Moses Lake that an employer could visit, independently confirmed through at least two of the company's own site, the Better Business Bureau, Yelp, Facebook, the Moses Lake Chamber of Commerce directory, or Washington's corporate registry — not a generic "we serve Moses Lake" landing page with a toll-free number and no real address. Roughly twenty candidates were reviewed in total; four cleared the bar.

The largest category of exclusion, and the one worth explaining in the most detail, is national staffing corporations with a genuine, staffed local branch office. This series established the standard in its Wichita coverage: a real branch office of a national staffing network is excluded not because anything is wrong with the branch or the company behind it, but because this list is specifically about locally-rooted businesses and disclosed local franchises, not national corporate branch networks. Mixing the two would obscure a genuinely useful distinction for a Moses Lake employer deciding who to call. Applying that same standard here:

  • Aerotek (1800 S Clover Dr, Suite 2) — a genuine physical branch, confirmed via a Columbia Basin Herald ribbon-cutting article, but a branch of a national corporate staffing network headquartered in Hanover, Maryland. Excluded on national-branch-network grounds. It's also newly opened (June 2026) and currently a one-person office.
  • BBSI (Moses Lake) (1550 S Pilgrim St, Suite B) — a real, staffed local branch, but a branch of Barrett Business Services Inc., a publicly traded company headquartered in Vancouver, Washington. Excluded on the same national-branch-network grounds. Worth noting factually: BBSI carries a D+ BBB rating with an unresolved complaint on file.
  • PeopleReady / Labor Ready — a branch of TrueBlue Inc., a national staffing network headquartered in Tacoma, Washington. Excluded on branch-network grounds; this research could not confirm the address found is still an active, current location.

A second category was dropped for a lack of verifiable evidence rather than any disqualifying trait. Hire Edge — Recruiting + Consulting has no verifiable street address anywhere in public records, so it was excluded as unverifiable. Elite Staffing, American Workforce Group, and NW Staffing Resources each turned up in searches, but none has a confirmed physical Moses Lake office; their real branches sit in Longview, Vancouver, Tacoma, and Chicago instead.

A third category was excluded on category grounds, not verification grounds — these are real, legitimate organizations, just not private staffing or recruiting businesses. WorkSource Central Basin / SkillSource is a government workforce-development center. OIC of Washington's Moses Lake site is a nonprofit job-training program for farmworkers. People For People (843 Kittleson Rd) is a 501(c)3 nonprofit providing transportation, meals, and employment and training programs. Columbia Basin Job Corps is a federal job-training program. The U.S. Army and Navy recruiting stations in Moses Lake are military recruiting offices, an entirely different category from civilian staffing and employment services. None of these were excluded because of any problem with the organization — they simply aren't the kind of business this list is measuring.

What to Check Before Hiring a Staffing Agency

  • Visit or call the Moses Lake address directly — several national firms with genuine local branches, and several more with no real local presence at all, show up in the same search results as the four locally-rooted agencies above
  • Ask for the markup percentage or placement fee in writing — none of these four publish rate cards, and quotes vary by role, credential, and placement type
  • For agricultural or food-processing roles, ask specifically about farm-labor contracting compliance — drug screening, background checks, and I-9 verification matter more in this labor category than in general office staffing
  • For healthcare roles, confirm current nurse licensing and any facility-specific credentialing directly with the agency — don't rely solely on a self-reported accreditation badge on a company's website
  • Confirm whether you're talking to a locally-owned business, a disclosed local franchise, or a branch of a national corporation — that distinction affects who you're actually contracting with and how decisions get made
  • Get temp-to-hire conversion terms in writing up front — the fee to convert a temp worker onto your own payroll is where surprises tend to show up later
  • Ask how the agency handles Moses Lake's seasonal agricultural demand swings — a generalist agency may not be as fluent in seasonal ag-labor sourcing as a specialist that works that cycle every year
