  • Verify the address independently — cross-check the agency's stated address against a Chamber of Commerce directory listing or another third-party source, not just the claim on the agency's own site

  • Press on self-reported statistics — client counts, growth percentages, and "years in business" figures quoted on an agency's own site should be treated as claims to verify, not settled facts
  • Check for a BBB profile or rating — none of the three companies profiled here carries a discoverable BBB rating, which isn't automatically disqualifying for a small local agency, but it means references and independent reviews carry more weight
  • Ask about a founding date that's inconsistent across sources — a one- or two-year discrepancy in a company's own story isn't necessarily a red flag, but it's worth a direct question rather than assuming the more flattering figure
  • Get pricing and scope in writing — none of the three publishes a rate card, so a written, scoped quote before signing is the only real protection
