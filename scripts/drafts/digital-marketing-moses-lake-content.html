Why Moses Lake's Digital Marketing Scene Stays Small

Moses Lake had an estimated population of roughly 26,969 to 27,000 in 2024 — a genuinely mid-sized Central Washington city, not a small town, but nowhere near the population density that supports a deep bench of digital-ad specialists. It sits inside a Grant County that has grown substantially, from about 50,000 residents in 1985 to more than 105,000 by 2025, according to Washington State Employment Security Department data. That's real, sustained growth, but it hasn't translated into a dense consumer-brand retail cluster of the kind that typically supports a deep bench of paid-ads and social-media specialists.

The core explanatory angle is the county's employer mix. Agriculture, forestry, and fishing remain Grant County's single largest employment sector by a wide margin, accounting for roughly 9,326 of the county's 43,169 total average annual jobs in 2024 per the state's own labor market reporting — but the economy has genuinely diversified beyond row crops and packing sheds into aerospace, advanced manufacturing, data centers, and battery-materials production. REC Silicon, a major global polysilicon producer, has long been one of Grant County's largest private-sector employers and taxpayers, though the company announced in late 2025 it would cease polysilicon production at its Moses Lake plant while continuing to operate its silane production line there — a reminder that even the area's largest legacy employers are in flux. Boeing has meanwhile grown into one of the county's largest employers in its own right, with roughly 1,000 employees locally by late 2023, and the Grant County Economic Development Council names newer entrants including Twelve (sustainable aviation fuel), Group14 Technologies, Sila Nanotechnologies, OneD, and Stoke Space Technologies as part of the county's growing aerospace and advanced-materials cluster.

Grant County International Airport, operated by the Port of Moses Lake, anchors much of that activity with a 13,500-foot runway — the 17th-longest in the United States — historically used for aerospace and flight testing, including by Boeing. Big Bend Community College, established in 1961, sits on the former Larson Air Force Base site and serves as the region's main workforce-training pipeline. Put together, a small population base combined with a heavy agricultural-and-industrial employer mix, rather than a dense consumer-brand retail cluster, means the local digital-marketing scene stays extremely thin — just two small, low-visibility shops turned up in this research, with national agencies filling the visibility gap through templated "Moses Lake" landing pages rather than genuine local offices.
