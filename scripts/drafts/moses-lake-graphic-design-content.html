Searching for graphic design companies in Moses Lake, WA turns up almost nothing once the print shops, sign companies, and out-of-state landing pages are filtered out. More than 20 candidates went through a two-source address check, a look at whether the business's own site actually names graphic design or branding as a real, demonstrated service, and a scan for any sign the "local" listing was actually headquartered somewhere else — Wenatchee, Kennewick, Yakima, even Phoenix and suburban Atlanta. Only two cleared every bar.

That's not a failure of research; it's a genuine feature of Moses Lake's economy. This is a Central Washington city of roughly 27,000 people built on agriculture, food processing, and an unusually heavy dose of aerospace and advanced manufacturing rather than a creative-agency cluster, and the numbers below explain why independent design studios are rare here while directory listings claiming a Moses Lake address are not. What follows is the short, honestly-verified list — plus the long list of names that didn't make it and exactly why.

Why Moses Lake's Design Market Looks the Way It Does

Moses Lake had an estimated population of roughly 26,969 in 2024, a genuinely mid-sized Central Washington city rather than a small town — and it sits inside a Grant County that has grown from about 50,000 residents in 1985 to more than 105,000 by 2025, according to Census Bureau and Washington State Employment Security Department data. That's real, sustained growth, not a one-time spike.

The county's economic base is still historically agricultural: agriculture, forestry, and fishing remain Grant County's single largest employment sector, accounting for roughly 9,326 of the county's 43,169 total average annual jobs in 2024 per the state's own labor market reporting. But the local economy has diversified well beyond row crops in the decades since, adding manufacturing, data centers, and technology employers alongside the packing sheds and processing plants.

The single most distinctive anchor is Grant County International Airport, built in 1942 as an Army training airfield and later operated as Larson Air Force Base until its closure in the mid-1960s. Its 13,500-foot runway is the 17th-longest in the United States, and the Port of Moses Lake, which operates the field today, has kept it in near-continuous use as a Boeing flight-test site — the 787, 747-8, 777X, and 737 MAX 10 have all logged test flights there — and as a designated NASA Space Shuttle alternate landing site. That's a genuinely unusual aerospace-testing pedigree for a city this size, and it sits alongside Big Bend Community College, a public two-year college established in 1961 that has occupied the former Larson Air Force Base campus since 1966 and now serves more than 5,400 students as the region's main workforce-training pipeline.

Notable local employers reflect that same industrial-agricultural mix: REC Silicon, a major polysilicon and solar-grade silicon manufacturer and one of Grant County's largest private employers; Moses Lake Industries, a supplier of ultra-high-purity semiconductor chemicals; Genie Industries; International Paper; J.R. Simplot; Basic American Foods; and National Frozen Foods. That combination — agriculture-processing plants sitting next to semiconductor-chemical suppliers and an active aerospace test range — gives Moses Lake a heavier industrial and technical employer base than a typical small city its size, and it's the most plausible explanation for why the market supports so few dedicated graphic-design studios. Demand here skews toward print, signage, and promotional-products shops and generalist marketing consultants rather than pure branding agencies, which is exactly the pattern that shows up in the excluded-candidates list below: multiple print, sign, and embroidery shops, several out-of-state templated landing pages, and only two genuine, verified design studios.

2. Backus Marketing & Design

Backus Marketing & Design is based at Suite 107, 102 E 3rd Ave — the Smith Martin Building, confirmed as a real, identifiable office via a Zillow commercial listing for the property, alongside Dun & Bradstreet, Manta, Yahoo Local, Local.com, and findstorenearme.us. A Better Business Bureau profile lists Jason Backus as owner; that BBB file happens to be filed under a regional Western Washington chapter rather than a Moses Lake-area one, which reads as a jurisdictional filing quirk rather than a location discrepancy — the business address itself is confirmed as Moses Lake by multiple independent directories. It's a small, family-owned operation (1–10 employees) that self-identifies as veteran-owned.

Named services run wider than a pure design studio: corporate identity, brand design and development, digital design and web development, and graphic design sit alongside marketing consulting, SEO, Google Ads management, and promotional products — making Backus a broader marketing generalist rather than a design-only shop, worth knowing going in. Its own testimonials disclose work for Young Living Essential Oils (a local representative), Bell Street Insurance (an Allstate agency), Precision Drone Solutions, Agri/Com Appraisals, Desert Rose Designs, Desert Graphics Inc., and the restaurant Michael's On The Lake.

Two things need a direct, upfront disclosure rather than a footnote. First, founding-year sources conflict: some directory listings put the company's start at 2014, while its own marketing language claims "18+ years" of experience — a gap that most likely reflects the owner's personal career length in the industry rather than the firm's actual age as a registered business, but it's a real discrepancy worth naming rather than smoothing over. Second, the company's own site advertises "20+ active clients" and an "81% client retention" rate; both are self-reported figures with no independent source behind them, and should be read as marketing claims rather than audited statistics.

Best fit for: a Moses Lake business that wants branding and web design bundled with ongoing digital marketing — SEO, Google Ads, and promotional products — from one local, veteran-owned team, rather than a pure design specialist.

Pricing: custom project quote; no published rate card.

How This List Was Verified

Roughly 20 candidate businesses were reviewed for this list. Each was checked against its own website first: does a services page name graphic design or branding as something the business actually does, in real detail, rather than a single word buried in a longer list of unrelated services? Address claims were then cross-checked against at least two independent sources — the business's own contact page, the Moses Lake Chamber of Commerce directory, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, or a comparable local or state business listing. Only two names passed both tests.

A handful of candidates failed on verifiability alone. G.Tieran Design, Marketing and Consultation is listed in the Chamber of Commerce directory, but no working website, LinkedIn profile, BBB record, Google Business listing, or any second independent source could be found — it's possibly a lapsed or inactive Chamber listing rather than an active business. Design Room appears only as a name inside aggregator search-result summaries, with no address, website, or independent confirmation behind it. Lola Ventures (also seen as Lola Ventures LLC) turns up in a single Yelp result with no verifiable address or business details; a separate, unrelated business also using the "Lola Ventures LLC" name is actually based in Kirkland, WA, which only adds to the confusion around the name. Ju CreativeContent has a Thumbtack-only freelancer profile with no verifiable business address — solo freelancers with no confirmed physical business location are excluded from this site's lists as a standing rule, regardless of how their portfolio looks.

Several more candidates are real, established Moses Lake businesses that were excluded on a service-match basis: they do real work, just not graphic design as a core, demonstrated offering. A & H Printers, Inc., at 1030 W Marina Dr, is a genuine 70-year local institution, but it's primarily a commercial printing company, with graphic design functioning as a secondary support service to its print jobs rather than a standalone practice. Signs Now, at 710 E Broadway, is a confirmed local business but a signage company rather than a design studio. Desert Graphics, at 1235 E Wheeler Rd, is a real, long-standing Moses Lake business focused on embroidery, promotional products, and screen printing. Shirt Builders, at 206 Fir St, is an apparel and screen-printing company. All four are legitimate businesses; none of them treats graphic design as its core, demonstrated service, which is the same standard applied consistently across every city list on this site.

A larger group failed on location entirely — real, often long-running design and print businesses, just not headquartered in Moses Lake. Blind Renaissance Inc. has been a real design firm since 1972, but it's headquartered in the Wenatchee Valley. Ridgeline Graphics is based at 962 Valley Mall Pkwy in East Wenatchee. Artmil's confirmed headquarters is 5601 W Clearwater Ave in Kennewick, part of the Tri-Cities. GilCova Design is headquartered in Wenatchee. Purple Coyote Printing is based in Ephrata. GO USA and Lightning Graphics are based in Wenatchee and Yakima respectively, and Fresh Print is based in Kennewick. Tishbyte Studios is based in Soap Lake — a distinct nearby town, not Moses Lake proper, even though the two are sometimes confused given their proximity.

The last group is the most consistent pattern across this site's research: out-of-state agencies running geo-targeted landing pages with no genuine local office. Toucan Design (toucandesign.net) is a nationwide templated directory and lead-generation network that auto-generates a near-identical page for every U.S. city, not a real local company. EZ Marketing is a national digital marketing agency running an SEO "locations" landing page that targets Moses Lake with no physical local office behind it. Passe Consulting's confirmed headquarters is Phoenix, AZ; its "Moses Lake North, WA" page is a geo-targeted landing page, not an office location. Appeal Design's confirmed headquarters is Sandy Springs, GA, in the Atlanta metro, founded in 2005; it runs a templated multi-city landing page that includes Moses Lake among dozens of other cities. And Seattle-based SEO agencies, including seattleseonow.com and similar operations, run geo-targeted "Moses Lake" landing pages with no local presence at all. None of these five were included, regardless of how polished their Moses Lake-specific page looked.

What to Check Before Hiring

  • Confirm the physical address independently — the majority of "Moses Lake graphic design" results reviewed for this list turned out to be Wenatchee, Kennewick, Yakima, or even out-of-state businesses using Moses Lake purely as an SEO target
  • Ask to see finished logo, print, or brand identity work — not just a portfolio page, since several excluded candidates were print shops, sign companies, or embroidery/promo-products vendors with design as a minor add-on
  • Get a written scope and price before work starts — neither of these two publishes a full public rate card
  • Clarify whether design is the core service or a bundled add-on — Backus is transparent about being a broader marketing firm, and that's fine as long as it matches what you're hiring for
  • Treat self-reported founding dates, "award-winning" language, and client-retention statistics as company-stated, not independently verified — this applies directly to both Boetger's "25+ years"/"award-winning" framing and Backus's "18+ years"/"81% client retention" claims
  • Ask who owns the final files — logo source files, fonts, and brand guidelines should transfer to you at project completion, regardless of which of the two you choose
  • Read current reviews directly before signing — don't rely on any single summary, including this one

The Bottom Line

Moses Lake's graphic design market is thin by design, not by accident of research — an agriculture-and-advanced-manufacturing economy anchored by a Boeing flight-test runway and a community college built on a former Air Force base produces print shops, sign companies, and generalist marketing consultants far more often than it produces standalone branding studios. Of roughly 20 candidates reviewed, only Michaelle Boetger Graphic Designs and Backus Marketing & Design held up under a two-source address check and a real look at whether design is a genuine, demonstrated service rather than a directory artifact.

Michaelle Boetger Graphic Designs is the more design-pure of the two, with nearly three decades of local history behind it. Backus Marketing & Design suits a business that wants design bundled with broader digital marketing from one local, veteran-owned team — just go in aware of its conflicting founding-year sources and self-reported client statistics. Whichever you choose, verify the address yourself and get scope and pricing in writing before signing — the same standard this list was built on.
